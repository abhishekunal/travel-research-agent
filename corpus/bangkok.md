# Bangkok

## Understand

Bangkok is Thailand's capital, with 10 million people in the city and 15 million across the metropolitan area. The city sits on the Chao Phraya River, 25 km inland from the Gulf of Thailand, and sprawls across both banks in a tangle of modern skyscrapers, royal palaces, Buddhist temples, elevated expressways, and narrow back alleys (*soi*). Its full ceremonial name is the longest city name in the world — locals shorten it to *Krung Thep* ("City of Angels"). Bangkok has been the Thai capital since 1782, when King Rama I moved it across the river from Thonburi. It is a Buddhist city — more than 400 temples (*wats*) — but one with an open, pragmatic cosmopolitanism shaped by waves of Chinese, Indian, and Western influence. Climate: tropical. Three seasons — cool, hot, rainy.

## Best Time to Visit

- **Cool season (November to February):** The best time to visit. Highs 28–32°C, low humidity, clear skies, pleasant evenings. Peak tourist season; book 2–3 months ahead for the December holidays.
- **Hot season (March to May):** Brutally hot (highs 35–40°C) and humid. Songkran (Thai New Year, April 13–15) is a national water-fight festival — fun to experience but transport is chaotic.
- **Rainy season (June to October):** Monsoon rains, usually afternoon downpours of 1–2 hours. Cheaper hotels, lush landscapes, few other tourists. September–October are the wettest months and risk street flooding.
- **Avoid:** Mid-April (Songkran shuts down the city and inflates prices); late September–early October if flooding risk matters for your plans.

## Districts

- **Rattanakosin (Old City):** The historical core on the river. Grand Palace, Wat Pho, Wat Phra Kaew, Khao San Road backpacker strip just north.
- **Sukhumvit:** Long east-west road with upscale neighborhoods along it. Trendy restaurants, bars, boutique hotels, nightlife. BTS Skytrain runs along it.
- **Silom and Sathorn:** Business district by day, dining and nightlife by night. Patpong night market.
- **Siam:** Shopping heart — massive malls (Siam Paragon, Siam Center, MBK, CentralWorld) around BTS Siam station.
- **Chinatown (Yaowarat):** Dense, old, Chinese-Thai neighborhood. Famous street food after dark. Gold shops by day.
- **Thonburi (west bank):** Across the river. Canals (*khlongs*), traditional neighborhoods, Wat Arun. Quieter.
- **Ari and Phahonyothin:** North of Siam. Hip residential, cafés, independent restaurants.

## Get in

- **Suvarnabhumi (BKK):** Main international airport, 30 km east of downtown. Airport Rail Link (direct to Phaya Thai, ~30 min, 45 THB). Taxi (public meter, 300–400 THB plus 70 THB expressway tolls to downtown). Grab (rideshare) available.
- **Don Mueang (DMK):** Older airport, 24 km north, used by low-cost airlines (AirAsia, Nok Air). Taxi (200–300 THB) or A1 bus to BTS Mo Chit (30 THB).

## Get around

- **BTS Skytrain:** Elevated train, two lines (Sukhumvit, Silom) crossing at Siam. Air-conditioned, modern, 16–59 THB per ride. The main tourist line.
- **MRT subway:** Underground, Blue and Purple lines. Connects to BTS at several stations.
- **Taxis:** Pink, yellow, and other bright colors. **Insist on the meter** — many drivers quote flat rates to tourists that are 2–3x fair. If they refuse, get out and hail another. 35 THB flag drop, 2 THB per km after.
- **Grab:** Rideshare app (also delivers food). Prices are fixed in-app; no haggling.
- **Tuk-tuks:** Three-wheeled open taxis. Fun but overcharge tourists aggressively; negotiate hard. Not actually cheaper than metered taxis.
- **Chao Phraya Express boats:** River bus from Nonthaburi to Sathorn Pier. Orange flag line (15 THB) is the local one; blue flag (40 THB) is tourist.
- **Khlong boats:** Narrow canal boats, cheap, locally used — Saen Saep line crosses east-west through the center.
- **Avoid driving.** Traffic is punishing; parking is scarce.

## See

- **Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew (Temple of the Emerald Buddha):** The most important royal/religious site. Strict dress code — shoulders and knees covered, no sheer fabrics. Open 08:30–15:30. Expect crowds.
- **Wat Pho (Temple of the Reclining Buddha):** 46-meter gold-leaf reclining Buddha. Also home of traditional Thai massage school.
- **Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn):** Across the river. Climbable central spire, best at sunset.
- **Chatuchak Weekend Market:** 15,000 stalls, Saturdays and Sundays. Everything from vintage clothing to pet goldfish.
- **Jim Thompson House:** Teakwood house of the American silk entrepreneur. Tranquil urban garden.
- **Lumpini Park:** Central city park. Early mornings bring tai chi groups and monitor lizards.
- **Erawan Shrine:** Hindu shrine on a busy street corner. Classical dancers perform for donations when visitors make offerings.
- **MBK Center or Chatuchak:** Shopping as spectacle.
- **Khao San Road:** Backpacker strip, mostly a memory of its 1990s self but still a late-night scene.

## Do

Concrete day activities and experiences:

- **Temple-hopping circuit.** Grand Palace, Wat Pho, Wat Arun (across the river by ferry, 4 THB) — a full morning. Finish with a Wat Pho massage.
- **Traditional Thai massage.** 300–500 THB for an hour at Wat Pho's school or a reputable neighborhood spot.
- **Street food crawl in Chinatown (Yaowarat).** Yaowarat Road comes alive after dark — pad thai, moo satay, oyster omelets, bird's nest soup.
- **Chao Phraya river boat ride.** Take the local orange-flag ferry one way and watch the city from the water.
- **Floating market day trip.** Damnoen Saduak is the famous one but very touristy; Amphawa (90 min away) is more authentic and operates afternoons on Fri/Sat/Sun.
- **Rooftop sunset drink.** Vertigo, Octave, Sky Bar — pricey but the view is the product.
- **Muay Thai match.** Rajadamnern or Lumpinee stadiums host championship fights several times a week.
- **Cooking class.** Half-day classes include market visit and 3–4 dishes. 1,200–2,000 THB.
- **Khlong (canal) boat tour.** 1–2 hours through the back canals of Thonburi — stilt houses, old temples, local life.
- **Markets by day.** Chatuchak (weekends), Pak Khlong Talat (flowers, 24 hours), Or Tor Kor (fresh market, cleaner than most).
- **Yaowarat gold shops.** Chinese-Thai gold-shop culture is a Chinatown experience in itself.

## Day Trips

- **Ayutthaya (1.5 hrs):** Former Thai capital, UNESCO-listed temple ruins. Train, bus, or private driver. Full day.
- **Kanchanaburi (2.5 hrs):** River Kwai, Bridge over the River Kwai, Death Railway, Erawan Falls. Overnight recommended but doable as a long day.
- **Amphawa Floating Market (1.5 hrs):** Weekend evening market on canals. Firefly boat tours at night.
- **Khao Yai National Park (2.5 hrs):** Thailand's oldest national park. Elephants, waterfalls, hiking. Need a car or tour.
- **Pattaya (2 hrs):** Beach resort with mixed reputation. Not usually the best use of a Bangkok trip.
- **Nakhon Pathom (1 hr):** World's tallest stupa (Phra Pathom Chedi).

## Eat & Drink

Bangkok's food scene is one of the best in the world, concentrated at street level and across every price tier.

**Dishes to try:**

- *Pad thai* — stir-fried rice noodles with egg, shrimp or chicken, bean sprouts.
- *Som tam* — green papaya salad; order *mai phet* (not spicy) if sensitive.
- *Tom yum goong* — hot and sour shrimp soup with lemongrass.
- *Khao mun gai* — Hainanese chicken rice. Lunch classic.
- *Khao soi* — northern Thai curry noodles with crispy noodle topping.
- *Massaman curry* — mild, Muslim-influenced curry with potato and peanut.
- *Moo satay* — grilled pork skewers with peanut sauce.
- *Mango sticky rice* — the dessert.
- *Boat noodles* — small, intense bowls of beef or pork noodle soup.
- *Pla phao* — grilled whole fish stuffed with lemongrass.

**Where to eat:**

- **Street food stalls:** Cheapest and often best. Follow the crowds. Food is cooked fresh in front of you. Chinatown and Soi 38 (now moved) are famous but every neighborhood has its stalls.
- **Food courts:** Mall food courts (Siam Paragon, Terminal 21) are air-conditioned, cheap, and clean. Buy a card at the counter, order from vendors.
- **Open-air shophouses:** The classic Thai restaurant — plastic chairs, tiled floors, menu on the wall.
- **Restaurants and rooftops:** Full range from local institutions to Michelin-starred.

**Etiquette:**

- Eat with a spoon (right hand) and fork (left, as a pusher). Chopsticks only for noodle soup.
- Thais share dishes — everyone eats a bit of everything.
- Don't finish every grain of rice on your plate — leaving a little is considered polite.
- Spice levels vary dramatically. "Thai spicy" at a local stand will be hotter than "spicy" at a tourist place.

**Drinks:**

- **Beer:** Chang, Singha, Leo are the big three. Cold, light, cheap (80–100 THB at a bar).
- **Thai iced coffee (*cha dum yen* for tea, *gafae yen* for coffee):** Super-sweet, from street vendors or coffee shops. The strong black Thai tea version is unmistakable.
- **Fruit shakes:** Blended on the street with ice and syrup, 60–80 THB.
- **Mekhong:** Thai whisky-like spirit, often mixed with Coke.
- **Cocktail bars:** The craft cocktail scene is strong — several Bangkok bars rank in Asia's 50 Best Bars.
- **Tap water is not safe to drink.** Bottled or filtered water everywhere; ice in reputable places is factory-made and safe.

## Budget and Costs

Approximate daily spend per person:

- **Budget:** ฿800–1,500/day. Hostel or budget guesthouse, street food, BTS/MRT only.
- **Mid-range:** ฿3,000–6,000/day. Mid-range hotel, mix of street food and sit-down meals, taxis.
- **Luxury:** ฿10,000+/day. 4-5 star hotel, Michelin-starred or rooftop dinners, private transport.

**Common costs:**

- BTS single ride: ฿16–59
- Taxi flag drop: ฿35
- Street-food meal: ฿50–100
- Sit-down Thai restaurant main: ฿150–350
- Beer at a bar: ฿80–200
- Thai massage (1 hr): ฿300–500
- Budget hotel/guesthouse: ฿600–1,200/night
- Mid-range hotel: ฿2,500–4,500/night
- Grand Palace entry: ฿500
- Muay Thai match: ฿1,000–2,500

## Practical Travel Information

- **Currency:** Thai baht (฿). Cash is standard for street food and tuk-tuks; cards accepted at mid-range and above.
- **ATMs:** Everywhere, but foreign-card withdrawals carry a 220 THB fee per transaction. Take out larger amounts less often.
- **Tipping:** Not expected but appreciated. Round up at restaurants; 50–100 THB for good service. No tip needed for taxi drivers unless they help with bags.
- **Dress code:**
  - **Temples:** Shoulders and knees covered, no sheer fabrics. Grand Palace is strict — sarongs and shirts can be rented at the entrance.
  - **Everyday:** Casual but neat. Thais dress better than visitors expect.
  - **Beach bars and nightlife:** Smart-casual; no flip-flops at better rooftops.
- **Electrical:** Type A, B, and C plugs. 220V, 50Hz. US devices need a step-down converter (not just an adapter) for anything with a heating element.
- **Internet:** Strong 4G/5G. Local SIMs (AIS, DTAC, True) are cheap — ~300 THB for a tourist SIM with weeks of data.
- **Monarchy and lèse-majesté law:** Insulting the royal family is a serious crime (up to 15 years in prison per count). Do not joke about it, even online. Stand when the royal anthem plays before cinema screenings.
- **Buddhism and respect:** Don't touch anyone's head. Don't point your feet at people, Buddha images, or shrines. Monks are not touched by women; if a woman needs to give an item to a monk, hand it to a man or place it on a surface for him to pick up.
- **Smiles:** The "Thai smile" has many meanings — not always pleasure. Keep your tone level even when annoyed.

## Stay safe

- **Scams:**
  - **"Temple is closed today":** A tuk-tuk driver stops you near the Grand Palace, says it's closed, offers to take you to another "temple" and a gem shop. The temple is open; walk past them.
  - **Gem scam:** Any pressure to buy jewelry as a tourist is a scam.
  - **Jet ski damage:** Rentals pre-damage boards and bill you for it.
- **Tuk-tuk overcharging:** The default. Negotiate before boarding; or just use metered taxis / Grab.
- **Street food hygiene:** Generally safe at busy stalls (high turnover = fresh food). Avoid pre-made items sitting at room temperature.
- **Traffic:** Extreme. Look both ways multiple times before crossing. Motorbike taxis weave the sidewalks.
- **Flooding:** Monsoon season (Sep–Oct) can produce street flooding; avoid low-lying areas during heavy rain.
- **Political demonstrations:** Occasional; usually peaceful but avoid.
- **Nighttime safety:** Bangkok is generally safe. Common sense in bars (don't accept drinks from strangers).

## Talk

- **Language:** Thai (tonal, five tones, non-Roman script).
- **English prevalence:** High in tourist areas and malls, moderate in business and hotels, low in street-food Bangkok. Written English on signs is common.
- **Useful phrases:** *Sawatdee krap/ka* (hello — men say *krap*, women *ka*); *khop khun krap/ka* (thank you); *mai phet* (not spicy); *tao rai?* (how much?); *mai aow* (I don't want).
- **The polite particle:** Add *krap* (male) or *ka* (female) to the end of sentences for politeness. Even "yes" becomes *krap/ka* alone.
- **The wai:** Thai greeting — palms pressed together, slight bow. Return a wai from a hotel staffer with a small nod; don't initiate wais to vendors or service staff (it's a hierarchy signal that can be awkward).
- **Face-saving:** Thai culture emphasizes *jai yen* (cool heart). Raising voice, visible anger, or public complaint rarely gets results and can shut down conversation.
