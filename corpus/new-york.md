# New York City

## Understand

New York City is the United States' largest city and its cultural, financial, and media capital, with roughly 8.5 million people in the city proper and 20 million across the metropolitan area. It sits where the Hudson River meets the Atlantic, spans five boroughs across islands and mainland, and has shaped global culture through finance (Wall Street), the arts (Broadway, SoHo galleries), media (publishing, television), and successive waves of immigration that give each neighborhood a distinct voice. The nickname "The City That Never Sleeps" is literal — the subway runs 24 hours, delis and diners stay open all night, and the pace never fully drops. The city has a humid subtropical climate with four strong seasons.

## Best Time to Visit

- **Spring (April to early June):** Mild, blooming parks, moderate crowds. Often the best season.
- **Fall (September to early November):** Crisp weather, fall colors in Central Park. Peak tourism.
- **Summer (late June to August):** Hot and humid (highs 28–33°C). Free outdoor events (Shakespeare in the Park, movies in Bryant Park, SummerStage), but humid and crowded.
- **Winter (December to March):** Cold with occasional snow. Holiday season (late Nov–early Jan) is magical but spikes prices. January and February are cheapest but genuinely cold.
- **Avoid:** The week between Christmas and New Year's — hotels double in price and the entire Midtown area is impassable.

## Boroughs

- **Manhattan:** The dense island most people picture. Financial District, SoHo, Greenwich Village, Midtown, Upper East/West Sides, Harlem.
- **Brooklyn:** Across the East River. Williamsburg (hip, young), DUMBO (riverfront, luxe), Park Slope (families), Bedford-Stuyvesant, Brooklyn Heights. The borough that defines modern cool.
- **Queens:** The most ethnically diverse area in the US. Astoria (Greek, now gentrifying), Flushing (Chinese), Jackson Heights (South Asian/Latin). The food capital.
- **The Bronx:** Yankees Stadium, the Bronx Zoo, strong Latin and Italian neighborhoods (Arthur Avenue is the real Little Italy).
- **Staten Island:** The ferry (free, with Statue of Liberty views) is the main reason most visitors come.

## Get in

Three airports serve NYC:

- **JFK (John F. Kennedy):** The main international airport. AirTrain connects to the A/E subway lines or LIRR. 45–75 min to Manhattan. Yellow taxi has a flat rate to Manhattan (~$70 before tip/tolls).
- **LaGuardia (LGA):** Closer (Queens), domestic-focused. Recently rebuilt. No direct subway — take the Q70 bus to the 7/E/F/M/R lines, or taxi/rideshare.
- **Newark (EWR):** In New Jersey. NJ Transit train to Penn Station, AirTrain Newark, or taxi/rideshare. Often has cheaper international fares.

## Get around

- **Subway:** 24 hours, 472 stations, 25 lines. Fast, affordable, essential. Pay with OMNY (contactless tap) or MetroCard. Fare caps weekly at ~$34, so swipe freely.
- **Buses:** Slower but useful for crosstown trips in Manhattan (streets go east-west, subway lines go north-south).
- **Walking:** Manhattan is grid-planned and very walkable. Blocks: 20 north-south equal roughly 1 mile; crosstown blocks are longer.
- **Taxis and rideshare:** Yellow cabs hailed on the street; Uber and Lyft everywhere. Traffic is often slower than the subway in Manhattan.
- **Citi Bike:** Docked bike share. Day passes available. Good for parks and bridge crossings.
- **Avoid driving.** Parking is $40–60/day, traffic is punishing, and the subway is faster.

## Do

Concrete day activities and experiences:

- **See a Broadway show.** TKTS Booth in Times Square sells same-day tickets at 20–50% off.
- **High Line walk.** Elevated former rail line turned park, Chelsea to Hudson Yards.
- **Central Park bike loop.** Full outer loop is ~6 miles; rent at Columbus Circle.
- **Museum crawl along the Upper East Side.** The Met, Guggenheim, Frick, Jewish Museum.
- **Chelsea Market and the High Line in one afternoon.**
- **Rooftop bar at sunset.** Not for the drink; for the skyline.
- **Shakespeare in the Park (summer).** Free, same-day line at the Delacorte in Central Park.
- **Smorgasburg (weekends, April–October).** Outdoor food market in Brooklyn.
- **Yankees or Mets game.** Even non-fans go for the atmosphere.
- **Comedy at UCB or the Comedy Cellar.** The city's standup scene is unmatched.
- **Walk the Brooklyn Bridge at dawn.** Fewer tourists, best photos.
- **Governor's Island (summer only).** Ferry from downtown Manhattan; car-free day escape.

## Day Trips

- **Hudson Valley (1–2 hrs):** Beacon (Dia:Beacon modern art), Cold Spring, Storm King sculpture park.
- **Philadelphia (1.5 hrs by Amtrak):** Independence Hall, cheesesteaks, Philly Museum of Art.
- **The Hamptons (2–3 hrs):** Summer beach towns on Long Island; expensive.
- **Fire Island (2 hrs including ferry):** Car-free beach community.
- **North Fork of Long Island (2 hrs):** Wineries, farm stands, lower-key than the Hamptons.
- **Boston (3.5 hrs by Amtrak):** Possible but long for one day.

## Eat & Drink

NYC's food scene is among the deepest in the world — defined less by refinement than by range.

**Iconic NYC foods:**

- **Pizza:** NY-style thin slice (dollar slice places are everywhere; Joe's and Prince Street Pizza are the famous ones). Also Neapolitan, Sicilian, and Detroit-style have strong representation.
- **Bagels with lox:** Hand-rolled, kettle-boiled. Cream cheese, lox, red onion, capers on an everything bagel is the classic.
- **Deli / pastrami on rye:** Jewish deli tradition — Katz's is the famous one.
- **Hot dogs:** From street carts (sabrett) or shops. "Dirty water dogs" are a thing.
- **Dim sum in Chinatown.**
- **Halal cart food:** Chicken over rice with white sauce and hot sauce. The 53rd & 6th cart started the genre.
- **Diner culture:** Open 24 hours. Order pancakes at 3 AM.

**Dining basics:**

- Tipping is 18–22% at sit-down restaurants.
- Reservations essential for popular places — Resy and OpenTable dominate.
- Dinner starts late by US standards (7:30–9:30 PM is prime time).
- Prix fixe lunch at famous restaurants (Restaurant Week in Jan/Feb and July) is the budget move.

**Drinks:**

- **Craft cocktails:** Speakeasy-style bars in the East Village, Lower East Side, and Brooklyn.
- **Dive bars:** The LES, East Village, and Bushwick preserve the tradition.
- **Specialty coffee:** Blue Bottle, Partners, La Cabra, Devoción — strong third-wave scene.
- **Beer:** Local breweries (Brooklyn Brewery, Threes, Other Half) and import-heavy bars.
- **Rooftops:** Pricey but the view is the product.

## Budget and Costs

Approximate daily spend per person:

- **Budget:** $100–150/day. Hostel or shared Airbnb, bagel/dollar slice meals, subway only.
- **Mid-range:** $250–400/day. Mid-range hotel or private Airbnb, sit-down meals, museum entries, occasional taxi.
- **Luxury:** $800+/day. 4- to 5-star hotel, Michelin-starred dining, Broadway orchestra seats, taxis.

**Common costs:**

- Subway single ride: $2.90 (weekly cap ~$34)
- Dollar slice pizza: $1.50–3
- Deli sandwich: $12–20
- Mid-range dinner entree: $25–45
- Craft cocktail: $18–22
- Budget hotel: $150–220/night
- Mid-range hotel: $280–500/night
- Broadway ticket: $100–300 (same-day TKTS 20–50% off)
- Museum entry (adult): $25–35 (The Met is "suggested" — pay what you want for NY residents)

## Practical Travel Information

- **Currency:** US dollar ($). Cards accepted virtually everywhere. Cash useful for food trucks and tipping.
- **Tipping:** 18–22% at restaurants, $1–2 per drink at bars, 15–20% for taxis and rideshare, $1–2 per bag for hotel bellhop, $2–5 for housekeeping per night.
- **Dress code:** Casual is fine almost anywhere. New Yorkers dress well but informally — jeans and a nice top gets you into most places. A handful of upscale restaurants require jackets.
- **Electrical:** Type A/B plugs, 120V, 60Hz.
- **Internet:** Strong 4G/5G everywhere. LinkNYC free wifi kiosks on sidewalks.
- **Public restrooms:** Scarce. Hotels, Starbucks, and museums are your best bets.
- **Subway etiquette:** Stand right on escalators, don't block doors, let people off before boarding. Don't make prolonged eye contact.
- **Weed:** Legal for adults 21+ as of 2021. Smell is everywhere; smoking in public is tolerated but technically still restricted in some settings.

## Stay safe

NYC is among the safer major US cities but awareness matters.

- **Street crime:** Overall low in Manhattan tourist areas and most of Brooklyn. More variable in parts of the Bronx, East New York, and other outer-borough neighborhoods — mostly affecting residents, not tourists.
- **Subway late at night:** Generally safe but less so after midnight; stand near the conductor's car or near others.
- **Scams:** Times Square costumed characters charge aggressively for photos. The "CD rapper" scam presses CDs into your hand and demands money. Just say no and walk.
- **Pickpocketing:** Rare but possible on crowded trains and Times Square.
- **Mental health visibility:** You will see people in distress on subways and streets. Give space; don't engage if someone is clearly disturbed.
- **Emergencies:** 911 for police/fire/medical. 311 for non-emergency city services.

## Talk

- **Language:** English. Spanish is widely spoken as a second language; many neighborhoods conduct daily life in other languages (Chinese in Flushing, Russian in Brighton Beach, etc.).
- **Accent and slang:** The "New York accent" is less common than TV suggests but survives among older working-class residents. Everyday slang includes "the city" (meaning Manhattan), "schlep" (a tedious trip), "stoop" (front steps), "regular" (coffee with milk and sugar).
- **Directness:** New Yorkers are famously blunt. This is not rudeness; it's efficiency. Match the energy and you'll get what you need faster.
- **"How are you?"** Not a real question. The answer is "Good, you?" and keep moving.
