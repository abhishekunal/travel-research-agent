# Barcelona

## Understand

Barcelona is Spain's second-largest city and the capital of the autonomous region of Catalonia, with 1.6 million people in the city and 5 million in the greater metropolitan area. It sits on the Mediterranean coast and combines a medieval Gothic core, a 19th-century grid of boutiques and modernist buildings, and a working waterfront. The city is Mediterranean in rhythm — long lunches, late dinners, a strong café and plaza culture — and Catalan in identity, with Catalan (not Spanish) as the historical regional language, visible on street signs and in local politics. Antoni Gaudí's unfinished Sagrada Família and his modernist buildings define the skyline and the tourist economy. Climate: Mediterranean — mild, dry winters and hot summers.

## Best Time to Visit

- **May to June:** Warm, sunny, pre-peak crowds. Beaches start being usable. Often the best season.
- **September to October:** Still warm, fewer crowds than summer, water still swimmable. Second-best choice.
- **July to August:** Hot (30–32°C), humid near the coast, extremely crowded, and many locals leave town. Hotels spike.
- **November to March:** Mild (12–16°C highs), less crowded, cheaper. Rare rain. Great for sightseeing without the heat.
- **Avoid:** Mid-August — half the city's shops and restaurants close for vacation; La Mercè (end of September) is a highlight if you want to catch a huge local festival.

## Districts

- **Ciutat Vella (Old Town):** The medieval core. Includes the Gothic Quarter (Barri Gòtic), El Born, Raval, and La Rambla pedestrian boulevard. Dense, atmospheric, tourist-heavy.
- **Eixample:** The 19th-century grid district designed by Cerdà. Chamfered octagonal corners, elegant modernist buildings, including most of Gaudí's work. Divided into Dreta (right, east) and Esquerra (left, west).
- **Gràcia:** North of Eixample. Village-like, bohemian, small plazas and bars. Where locals go.
- **Barceloneta:** The beach neighborhood built on reclaimed land. Narrow lanes, seafood restaurants, waterfront.
- **Poble-sec and Montjuïc:** South and west. Montjuïc is a hill with castle, museums, botanical garden, and 1992 Olympic venues.
- **Poblenou (22@ district):** Former industrial area turned tech and design hub. Beaches continue up here with fewer crowds.
- **Sarrià-Sant Gervasi and Pedralbes:** Upscale residential hills to the northwest.

## Get in

- **Barcelona-El Prat Airport (BCN):** 12 km from downtown. Two terminals (T1 for major carriers, T2 for low-cost), connected by free shuttle.
- **To the city:** Aerobus (direct to Plaça Catalunya, 35 min, €5.90), R2 Nord train (connects to the metro at Sants, 25 min), metro L9 Sud, or taxi (~€30–35 flat rate).
- **Train:** High-speed AVE trains run from Barcelona Sants to Madrid in 2.5 hours and connect to the French TGV network.

## Get around

- **Metro:** Clean, fast, air-conditioned. 11 lines, extensive network. The T-casual card (10 single rides) is the budget option; Hola BCN cards cover unlimited rides for 2–5 days.
- **Buses and trams:** Supplement the metro, especially for hills and the periphery.
- **Walking:** The Old Town is best walked. Eixample is grid-planned so walking is predictable; blocks are ~130m.
- **Bicing (bike share):** Resident-only; tourists can't register. Private rentals and e-scooters are everywhere.
- **Taxis:** Black and yellow, metered, affordable. Hail on the street or at taxi stands.
- **Avoid driving.** One-way streets, scarce parking, pedestrianized zones.

## See

- **Sagrada Família:** Gaudí's unfinished basilica. Book tickets online weeks ahead — slots sell out.
- **Park Güell:** Gaudí's hilltop park. The famous mosaic terrace is in a paid "monumental zone"; the rest is free. Book ahead.
- **Casa Batlló** and **Casa Milà (La Pedrera):** Gaudí's two modernist apartment buildings on Passeig de Gràcia.
- **Barcelona Cathedral:** 13th-century Gothic cathedral in the Gothic Quarter.
- **La Rambla:** The pedestrian boulevard. Famous but tourist-heavy and pickpocket-dense.
- **Mercat de la Boqueria:** Market off La Rambla. Buy produce and jamón; eat at the counters.
- **Picasso Museum:** El Born. Early Picasso works, including his Barcelona period.
- **Palau de la Música Catalana:** Modernist concert hall with a stained-glass skylight.
- **Camp Nou:** FC Barcelona's stadium. Tours when there is no match.
- **Montjuïc:** Castle, Olympic Stadium, Fundació Joan Miró, Magic Fountain shows.

## Do

Concrete day activities and experiences:

- **Modernisme walk through Eixample.** Follow a self-guided route past the main Gaudí and Domènech i Montaner buildings.
- **Tapas crawl in El Born or Gràcia.** Hop between two or three bars, one or two bites and a drink at each.
- **Beach half-day at Barceloneta or Bogatell.** Rent a sun lounger (~€15); beach bars (*xiringuitos*) serve drinks and seafood.
- **Flamenco show.** Flamenco isn't Catalan originally, but intimate venues host nightly performances. Pick a smaller venue over a big tourist spectacle.
- **Sunset at Bunkers del Carmel.** The best view of the city, from a hill. Bring wine and a sweater.
- **Day on Montjuïc.** Castle, cable car, Fundació Joan Miró, Magic Fountain show after dark.
- **La Boqueria breakfast.** Early (before 10) to avoid the tour groups.
- **Tibidabo.** Hilltop amusement park with a church and panoramic views. The old funicular is part of the fun.
- **FC Barcelona match (season Aug–May).** Even non-fans find it electric.
- **Festes de Gràcia (mid-August) or La Mercè (September 24):** Catch a local festival if your dates line up.
- **Catalan folk dance (sardana).** Sunday mornings in front of the cathedral, locals form open circles — you can join.

## Day Trips

- **Montserrat (1 hr):** Mountain monastery with jagged rock peaks, boys' choir performs around 1 PM most days.
- **Sitges (40 min):** Seaside town with 17 beaches, LGBT-friendly, grand old houses. Beach day or evening dinner.
- **Girona (40 min by AVE train):** Walled medieval town, Jewish Quarter, Game of Thrones filming locations.
- **Figueres (1 hr by AVE):** Dalí Theatre-Museum — Dalí's hometown, his own museum of his work.
- **Tarragona (1 hr by train):** Roman ruins (amphitheater by the sea), UNESCO-listed, less crowded than Barcelona.
- **Costa Brava (1–2 hrs):** Rugged northern coastline with cove beaches; Cadaqués (Dalí) and Tossa de Mar are the postcard towns.

## Eat & Drink

Barcelona's food is Catalan, not generic Spanish — the regional distinction matters.

**Catalan dishes to try:**

- *Pa amb tomàquet* — toasted bread rubbed with ripe tomato, olive oil, salt. Appears at every meal.
- *Esqueixada* — salt-cod salad with tomato, onion, olives.
- *Fideuà* — like paella but with short noodles instead of rice.
- *Arròs negre* — black rice with squid ink.
- *Botifarra amb mongetes* — Catalan sausage with white beans.
- *Calçots* (winter only) — grilled spring onions with romesco sauce; messy and seasonal.
- *Crema catalana* — Catalan crème brûlée.

**Dining basics:**

- **Timing:** Lunch is the big meal, 2–4 PM. Dinner starts 9 PM at the earliest; many restaurants don't open until 8:30. Siesta hours (4–8 PM) see many places closed.
- **Menú del día:** Fixed-price multi-course lunch, usually €14–20. Enormous value.
- **Tapas vs pintxos:** Tapas are small plates you order; pintxos are small bites on bread with a toothpick, often laid out on the counter at Basque-style bars. Count your toothpicks at the end.
- **Tipping:** 5–10% is appreciated but not expected; round up or leave coins.

**Drinks:**

- *Vermut* — vermouth, served with soda and an orange slice. The pre-lunch ritual (*fer el vermut*) is still common on Sundays.
- *Cava* — Catalan sparkling wine. Cheaper and often better than mid-range champagne.
- *Clara* — beer with lemon soda, hugely popular in summer.
- *Café con leche* — coffee with milk, Catalan version of a cortado is a *tallat*.
- Catalan wine regions (Priorat, Montsant, Penedès) are serious; ask for a local red.

## Budget and Costs

Approximate daily spend per person:

- **Budget:** €60–90/day. Hostel, menú del día for lunch, tapas and beer for dinner, metro only.
- **Mid-range:** €120–200/day. Mid-range hotel or Airbnb, mix of casual and sit-down meals, museum entries, Sagrada Família ticket.
- **Luxury:** €350+/day. Hotel in Eixample or Barceloneta, tasting-menu dinners, private tours.

**Common costs:**

- Metro single ride: €2.65
- T-casual (10-ride card): €12.55
- Menú del día lunch: €14–22
- Beer: €3–5
- Vermut: €3–5
- Mid-range dinner main: €15–25
- Budget hotel: €90–130/night
- Mid-range hotel: €180–280/night
- Sagrada Família ticket: €26–40
- Park Güell monumental zone: €13–17

## Practical Travel Information

- **Currency:** Euro (€). Cards widely accepted; small bars and markets still often cash.
- **Tipping:** Not expected. Rounding up or leaving a euro or two is appreciated.
- **Dress code:** Smart-casual at nice restaurants. Beach attire for the beach only — not acceptable on the metro or in restaurants.
- **Siesta culture:** Many shops close 2–5 PM; big chain stores and tourist-area restaurants stay open.
- **Electrical:** Type C and F plugs, 230V, 50Hz. US devices need an adapter.
- **Internet:** Strong 4G/5G. European eSIMs or local prepaid SIMs cheap and easy.
- **Water:** Tap water is safe, though many locals prefer bottled for taste. Fountains in parks are drinkable.
- **Shop hours:** Many smaller shops closed Sundays entirely.

## Stay safe

- **Pickpocketing:** Barcelona's most-reported problem. La Rambla, metro stations (especially Sants), and the beach are the hotspots. Front pockets, zipped bags, never put a phone on a cafe table.
- **Scams:** The "fake petition" distraction, the "spilled sauce" distraction, pickpockets working in groups.
- **Beach theft:** Bags stolen the moment you turn to swim. Go in shifts if alone, or use a beach safe.
- **Fake police:** Real police won't demand to see your wallet on the street. Walk away.
- **Night safety:** The city is overall safe; stick to well-lit areas late at night, especially around Raval and the lower end of La Rambla.
- **Protests:** Occasional Catalan independence demonstrations are usually peaceful but can disrupt transit.

## Talk

- **Languages:** Catalan and Spanish are both official. Signs are usually in Catalan, often with Spanish and English.
- **Which to use:** Spanish works everywhere. Trying a few words of Catalan is appreciated — "bon dia" (good day), "si us plau" (please), "gràcies" (thanks).
- **English prevalence:** High in tourist areas, strong among younger people, moderate elsewhere. More widely spoken than in Madrid.
- **Catalan vs Spanish:** Catalan is not a dialect — it's a separate Romance language, closer to Occitan than to Castilian. Local pride in it is strong and political.
- **Formality:** Spanish *tú* (informal) is widely used; *usted* (formal) reserved for elderly and formal settings.
