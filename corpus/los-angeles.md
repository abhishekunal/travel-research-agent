# Los Angeles

## Understand

Los Angeles is the second-largest city in the United States and the center of a metropolitan area of 13 million people spread across a basin bordered by mountains and the Pacific Ocean. The city itself holds 4 million; what visitors call "LA" is really a loose constellation of 88 municipalities (Beverly Hills, Santa Monica, Culver City, Pasadena, and more) stitched together by freeways. There is no single downtown — the city has multiple centers, and the sprawl is the point rather than a flaw. LA was founded as a Spanish mission in 1781 (*El Pueblo de Nuestra Señora la Reina de los Ángeles*) and became a US city after the Mexican-American War; those roots remain in Spanish place names and in the city's largest ethnic group, which is Mexican-American. LA is also the global home of film and television (Hollywood), a major music industry hub, and increasingly, aerospace and tech. Climate: Mediterranean — mild, sunny, dry most of the year.

## Best Time to Visit

- **March to May (spring):** Mild (highs 20–24°C), green hills from winter rain, moderate crowds. Often the best season.
- **September to November (fall):** Warm, dry, less crowded than summer. Watch for wildfire smoke in September–October.
- **June to August (summer):** Warm but tempered near the coast; valleys inland get genuinely hot. June Gloom — morning marine layer — is a real thing at the beach through June. Peak tourist season; prices spike.
- **December to February (winter):** Cool (highs 18–20°C), rainy by LA standards. Mild compared to anywhere else in the US. Lots of film-award events in January/February.
- **Avoid:** Oscars week (late February–early March) and major convention weeks (E3, Anime Expo in July if either returns) if you want lower hotel prices.

## Regions and Neighborhoods

LA's geography is the biggest tourist adjustment. Six broad zones to know:

- **Downtown (DTLA):** Civic center, Arts District, Little Tokyo, historic core. Grand Central Market, Walt Disney Concert Hall, The Broad. Recently revitalized; good for a day of walking.
- **Hollywood and West Hollywood (WeHo):** Hollywood Walk of Fame, TCL Chinese Theatre, Sunset Strip, nightlife, the music industry. Hollywood proper is more tourist-dense; WeHo is more polished.
- **Mid-Wilshire and Koreatown:** The Miracle Mile museum row (LACMA, La Brea Tar Pits, Petersen Automotive). Koreatown is the city's most 24-hour neighborhood.
- **The Westside:** Beverly Hills, Culver City, Venice, Santa Monica, Brentwood, Westwood (UCLA). Beaches, upscale shopping, Getty Center.
- **The Valleys:**
  - **San Fernando Valley** (north, over the Hollywood Hills): Studio City, Burbank (where most "Hollywood" actually films), Van Nuys. Suburban feel.
  - **San Gabriel Valley** (east): Pasadena, Alhambra, Monterey Park. Pasadena has the Rose Bowl and Huntington Library; the San Gabriel Valley has the country's best Chinese food.
- **South Bay and Long Beach:** Beach cities south of LAX — Manhattan Beach, Hermosa, Redondo, down to Long Beach and the Queen Mary.
- **East LA and Boyle Heights:** Mexican-American heart of the city. Mariachi Plaza, street food, authentic taquerías.

## Get in

Five commercial airports serve the LA area:

- **LAX (Los Angeles International):** Main airport. Notoriously congested. Metro K Line now connects to the broader rail network; LAX/Metro Transit Center opened recently. Rideshare pickup is at LAX-it lot (free shuttle from terminals).
- **BUR (Hollywood Burbank):** Easier, smaller, in the San Fernando Valley. 20 min to Hollywood on a good day; frequently preferred for domestic travel.
- **LGB (Long Beach):** Small, pleasant, good for South Bay and Orange County destinations.
- **SNA (John Wayne, Orange County):** South of LA, closer to Anaheim/Disneyland than to LA proper.
- **ONT (Ontario):** East of LA, growing. Good for Inland Empire and Palm Springs.

## Get around

- **Car:** The honest default. Rent a car for anything beyond one neighborhood a day. Freeway system — the 405, 101, 10, 110 — is the circulatory system. Download Waze or Google Maps for real-time traffic.
- **Rideshare:** Uber and Lyft are everywhere. Prices surge during peak times and events. Reasonable for distances under ~15 miles.
- **Metro Rail:** More useful than reputation suggests. The B Line (Red) runs from Downtown to North Hollywood. The E Line (Expo) runs Downtown to Santa Monica. The A Line goes south to Long Beach. The K Line connects LAX to the Expo Line.
- **Buses:** Metro, DASH (downtown circulator), Big Blue Bus (Santa Monica), Culver CityBus. Slower but functional for specific routes.
- **Walking:** Possible within neighborhoods — Santa Monica, Venice, DTLA, Hollywood, parts of WeHo. Walking between neighborhoods is impractical.
- **Scooters and bikes:** Available in beach neighborhoods and DTLA; mostly a short-trip option.
- **Avoid rush hour.** 7–10 AM and 3–7 PM freeway driving can double or triple travel time. Plan around it.

## See

- **Griffith Observatory and Griffith Park:** Classic LA view of the Hollywood Sign, downtown skyline, and the Pacific. Free entry; plan around sunset.
- **Hollywood Sign:** Best viewed from Griffith Park (far view) or from Lake Hollywood Park / Mt Hollywood Trail (closer views). Don't try to drive to it.
- **Walk of Fame and TCL Chinese Theatre:** The Hollywood Boulevard tourist strip. Mildly disappointing in person but iconic.
- **The Getty Center:** Hilltop art museum designed by Richard Meier. Free admission (parking fee). Half-day minimum.
- **The Getty Villa (Pacific Palisades):** Antiquities. Free, timed reservation.
- **LACMA (Los Angeles County Museum of Art):** The urban-lights sculpture out front is the LA photo op.
- **The Broad:** Free (reservations recommended) contemporary art museum downtown.
- **Santa Monica Pier and Venice Beach:** Carnival pier, beach, bike path from Santa Monica to Venice, boardwalk muscle beach, Abbott Kinney Boulevard.
- **Universal Studios Hollywood:** The theme park, not the actual studio.
- **Walt Disney Concert Hall and The Grand Avenue arts corridor:** Frank Gehry architecture; worth a walk-around.
- **Grand Central Market:** 1917 downtown food hall. Lunch spot and a historical destination.

## Do

Concrete day activities and experiences:

- **Griffith Park hike + Observatory evening.** Hike up to the Observatory for sunset. Multiple trailheads — Fern Dell is shaded; Western Canyon climbs faster.
- **Beach day in Santa Monica or Venice.** Rent a cruiser bike at the pier; ride the car-free beach path south.
- **Studio tour.** Warner Bros Studio Tour in Burbank is the serious one; Universal is attached to the theme park.
- **Mulholland Drive scenic drive.** From the 101 at Cahuenga Pass to the 405, with city and valley views.
- **Grand Central Market lunch + Walk the historic Broadway corridor.**
- **Rooftop sunset.** Perch (DTLA), Mama Shelter (Hollywood), Elephante (Santa Monica).
- **Food truck crawl.** Grand Central Market by day; Smorgasburg on Sundays; curated truck events.
- **Taco tour of East LA.** Guisados, Mariscos Jalisco, Carnitas El Momo, Leo's Tacos Truck.
- **Rose Bowl Flea Market (second Sunday of each month).** Pasadena. Early bird is the real event.
- **Dodgers game (season April–October).** Dodger Stadium, 1962-era, mountain views.
- **Live music.** The Hollywood Bowl in summer; the Wiltern, the Troubadour, the Greek Theatre year-round.
- **Comedy.** The Comedy Store, Largo at the Coronet, Upright Citizens Brigade.
- **Pretend you live here.** Spend a Saturday in Silver Lake — coffee at Café de Leche, LPs at Rockaway, dinner at Pine & Crane.

## Day Trips

- **Santa Barbara (90 min):** Spanish colonial architecture, wine country, beach town. State Street for strolling.
- **Palm Springs (2 hrs):** Mid-century modern architecture, pool culture, Joshua Tree gateway. Hot in summer.
- **Joshua Tree National Park (2–2.5 hrs):** Desert, boulders, Yucca brevifolia (Joshua trees). Spring and fall are the best seasons.
- **Catalina Island (1 hr by ferry from Long Beach):** Avalon town, snorkeling, no-car island. Good overnight.
- **Big Bear Lake (2.5 hrs):** Mountain lake town — hiking in summer, skiing in winter.
- **Ojai (1.5 hrs):** Boutique town north of Ventura; artsy, slow, mountain-ringed.
- **Big Sur (4+ hrs):** Spectacular coast but too far for a day trip; overnight.
- **San Diego (2 hrs):** Beaches, Balboa Park, Mexican-American food. Easy day or overnight.

## Eat & Drink

LA's food scene is defined by immigrant cuisines and casual-first culture. The best meals here are often $15 at a strip-mall counter.

**Iconic LA foods:**

- **Mexican:** Tacos (al pastor, carnitas, carne asada, mariscos), tortas, pupusas (Salvadoran, Pico-Union), chile verde, mole. East LA, Boyle Heights, and the San Gabriel Valley are the main zones.
- **Korean:** Korean BBQ, soondubu, bibimbap, Korean fried chicken. Koreatown is a full dining universe.
- **Chinese (San Gabriel Valley):** Monterey Park, Alhambra, Rowland Heights — regional Chinese cuisines (Sichuan, Xi'an, Shanghainese) at a level unmatched in the US.
- **Thai:** East Hollywood's Thai Town — authentic northern Thai (khao soi, laab).
- **Japanese:** Little Tokyo downtown; also strong ramen and izakaya scene in Sawtelle (Westside) and the Valley.
- **Food trucks:** Kogi (Korean-Mexican) started the movement in 2008; the whole food-truck ecosystem followed.
- **In-N-Out:** The regional fast-food institution — order animal-style; cash or card.
- **Classic American:** Diners (Pann's, Pacific Dining Car), French dip (Philippe the Original, Cole's), Southern (Dulan's).

**Dining basics:**

- Tipping is 18–22% at sit-down restaurants, 10–20% at counter spots.
- Reservations are easy to get except at the top-of-moment places.
- Dinner is earlier than NYC or SF — 7–9 PM is typical.
- Many restaurants emphasize patio seating given the weather.

**Drinks:**

- **Coffee:** LA has a strong third-wave scene. Intelligentsia (originally Chicago), G&B Coffee, Verve, Go Get Em Tiger, Blue Bottle.
- **Craft cocktails:** DTLA (Arts District, Perch, The Varnish), WeHo, Santa Monica.
- **Wine bars:** Especially in Silver Lake, Echo Park, and Santa Monica.
- **Craft beer:** Strong local scene — Smog City, Modern Times, Angel City, Mumford.
- **Wellness drinks:** Juice bars, kombucha on tap, matcha everywhere. The stereotype is real.

- **Budget:** $90–140/day. Hostel or budget motel, taco-truck meals, Metro + occasional rideshare.

## Practical Travel Information

- **Currency:** US dollar ($). Cards everywhere; cash useful for food trucks and tipping.
- **Tipping:** 18–22% restaurants; $1–2 per drink at bars; 15–20% rideshare and taxis; $1–2 per bag for hotel bellhop.
- **Dress code:** Casual is the baseline — LA is less dressy than NYC. Jeans and nicer sneakers get you most places. Beachy vibes OK in beach towns; a step up for Hollywood/WeHo clubs and higher-end dining.
- **Electrical:** Type A/B plugs, 120V, 60Hz.
- **Internet:** Strong 4G/5G citywide; patchy in Griffith Park and some canyons.
- **Weed:** Legal for adults 21+. Dispensaries abundant. Public consumption still illegal.
- **Driving tips:**
  - Pedestrians have right of way at crosswalks (and in LA, they'll walk assuming it).
  - "The 405," "the 101" — Southern Californians use "the" before freeway numbers.
  - Right turn on red is legal after a complete stop unless signed otherwise.
  - No jaywalking — technically legal now statewide, but still enforced at tourist spots.
- **Parking:** Street parking signs are complicated. Read every sign on the pole; one no-parking rule anywhere invalidates the whole stretch. Garages range $5–40.
- **Beach water:** Check LA County beach water quality reports; some beaches (especially after rain) have advisories.

## Stay safe

- **Driving:** The real risk. Aggressive drivers, long freeway speeds, extensive distracted driving.
- **Earthquakes:** Possible at any time. Drop/cover/hold on. California building codes are strict; damage from a modest quake is unlikely.
- **Wildfires:** September–December in the surrounding hills. Red flag warnings mean high fire risk; smoke can degrade air quality citywide.
- **Property crime:** Car break-ins are the most common tourist crime. Don't leave anything visible in a parked car — not a bag, not a jacket, not a charger. Rental cars are targeted.
- **Homelessness and street camps:** Visible in parts of downtown (Skid Row), Hollywood, and under some freeway overpasses. Give space; most are not dangerous.
- **Nighttime:** Hollywood Boulevard, Downtown east of Spring Street, and parts of MacArthur Park are areas to be aware in after dark. The Westside and Valley are generally safe.
- **Beach safety:** Rip currents at Pacific beaches; swim near lifeguards. Water is cold year-round.

## Talk

- **Language:** English. Spanish is the universal second language — nearly all signage in bilingual, many service workers speak Spanish primarily. Korean, Mandarin, Cantonese, Thai, Armenian, Farsi, Vietnamese, Tagalog all have major presences.
- **The LA accent:** More subtle than NY or Boston; a mild California drawl, uptalk ("rising intonation"), vocal fry. "The" before freeway numbers is the real tell.
- **Local slang:** "The Valley" (San Fernando or San Gabriel — specify), "the Westside" (west of La Brea or thereabouts), "the beach" (as destination, not description), "the industry" (film/TV).
- **Pace:** Slower than NYC. Expect small talk at the counter and in Ubers. "Living the dream" is an actual answer to "How are you?"
- **Driving as culture:** Conversations happen in cars, dates happen across neighborhoods. The commute shapes the day — asking "where are you?" is less about location than about transit time.
