# Paris

## Understand

Paris is France's capital and one of Europe's largest urban areas — 2.1 million people in the city proper and 12 million across the metropolitan area. It sits on the Seine river, which splits the city into Right Bank (Rive Droite, more commercial) and Left Bank (Rive Gauche, more academic and literary), connected by 37 bridges across the city center. Modern Paris was shaped by Haussmann's 19th-century rebuilding, which cut long boulevards through the medieval city, imposed a uniform building style (six floors, zinc roofs, wrought-iron balconies), and gave the city the look most visitors arrive expecting. Paris is organized into 20 arrondissements numbered in a clockwise spiral starting from the Louvre. Climate: oceanic — mild winters, warm summers, rain year-round.

## Best Time to Visit

- **April to June:** Spring. Mild, longer days, flowers, moderate crowds. The classic Paris season.
- **September to October:** Autumn. Cool, still-pleasant, fewer tourists, cultural season reopens (museums refresh, galleries open shows).
- **July to August:** Hot (highs 25–30°C with increasing heat waves). Many Parisians leave town; small restaurants and shops close for *les grandes vacances*, especially the first three weeks of August.
- **November to March:** Cool to cold (highs 7–12°C), often grey. Rain, rarely snow. Christmas lights are magnificent (mid-November to early January). Cheapest for flights outside the holiday weeks.
- **Avoid:** Mid-August (closures), mid-December through early January (price spikes), and the weeks around French strikes (announced in advance — check before booking).

## Arrondissements

Paris's 20 districts spiral out from the center. A quick orientation:

- **1st–4th (Center, Right Bank):** The historic core. Louvre, Palais-Royal, Les Halles, Île de la Cité (Notre-Dame), Marais (3rd and 4th — the most fashionable district, gay village, historic Jewish quarter).
- **5th–6th (Left Bank, Center):** The Latin Quarter (5th — Sorbonne, Panthéon) and Saint-Germain-des-Prés (6th — literary cafés, galleries).
- **7th (Left Bank):** Eiffel Tower, Invalides, Musée d'Orsay. Elegant residential.
- **8th:** Champs-Élysées, Arc de Triomphe, Élysée Palace. Grand but commercial.
- **9th:** Opéra Garnier, department stores (Galeries Lafayette, Printemps). SoPi (South Pigalle) is the hip subset.
- **10th:** Canal Saint-Martin, Gare du Nord. Youthful, trendy, improving.
- **11th:** Bastille to République. Nightlife capital, hundreds of bars and small restaurants.
- **18th:** Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur. Picturesque, tourist-dense.
- **19th–20th:** Belleville, Père-Lachaise, Buttes-Chaumont park. More diverse, cheaper, less touristy.

## Get in

- **Charles de Gaulle (CDG):** Main international airport, 25 km northeast of Paris. RER B train to central Paris (~45 min, €11.80). Roissybus to Opéra (~1 hr, €16.60). Taxi flat rate ~€53 to Right Bank, €58 to Left Bank.
- **Orly (ORY):** Smaller, 13 km south. OrlyBus to Denfert-Rochereau (~30 min, €11.50), Orlyval + RER B, or taxi.
- **Train:** Eurostar from London to Gare du Nord (2h 20m). TGV from most French cities and to Brussels, Amsterdam, Barcelona.

## Get around

- **Métro:** 16 lines, dense coverage, runs 05:30–01:15 (02:15 Fri/Sat). Carnet of 10 tickets €17.35; day/week passes via Navigo card. Stations often lack elevators — hard with heavy luggage.
- **RER:** Faster suburban trains that cross the city with fewer stops; useful for CDG, Versailles, La Défense.
- **Buses:** Scenic alternative; night buses (Noctilien) cover gaps.
- **Walking:** Paris rewards walking — a compact center, consistent block scale. Watch for drivers who don't always yield at crosswalks.
- **Vélib':** Docked bike share, 1,400 stations. Day pass €5. Lots of bike lanes added in the past five years.
- **Taxis:** Metered, booked via Alpha, G7, or Uber. Fewer street-hailed than NYC.
- **Avoid driving.** Narrow streets, scarce and expensive parking, confusing one-ways.

## See

- **Eiffel Tower:** Book ahead for elevator; stairs don't need advance booking and skip the longest line. Illuminated with sparkling lights for 5 min at each hour after dark.
- **Louvre:** The world's most-visited museum. Mona Lisa is crowded; the lesser-known rooms are the real reward. Buy timed tickets online.
- **Musée d'Orsay:** Impressionists and 19th-century French art in a Belle Époque train station. Many prefer it to the Louvre.
- **Notre-Dame:** Reopened in December 2024 after the 2019 fire. Still check current visiting rules.
- **Sainte-Chapelle:** Gothic chapel with the city's most spectacular stained glass. Small; often has a line.
- **Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur:** Walk up through the artist squares; crowded but iconic.
- **Arc de Triomphe:** Climb the top for the view down the twelve avenues radiating from Place Charles de Gaulle.
- **Centre Pompidou:** Modern art in the famous inside-out building.
- **Musée Rodin:** Garden and mansion of Rodin's sculptures. Underrated.
- **Catacombs:** 2 km of tunnels lined with the bones of 6 million Parisians. Book ahead.

## Do

Concrete day activities and experiences:

- **Walk the Seine from Pont Neuf to Pont Alexandre III.** Especially at dusk.
- **Picnic in Luxembourg Gardens.** Buy bread, cheese, and wine on the way; chairs beside the fountain are free.
- **Covered-passage walk through the 2nd and 9th.** Galerie Vivienne, Passage des Panoramas, Passage Jouffroy. Pre-department-store shopping arcades from the 19th century.
- **Marais wandering.** Boutique shopping, Jewish Quarter around Rue des Rosiers, falafel line at l'As.
- **Montmartre at dawn.** Beat the crowds to Place du Tertre.
- **Père-Lachaise Cemetery.** Jim Morrison, Oscar Wilde, Chopin, Edith Piaf. A full afternoon of a hilly park full of elaborate tombs.
- **Canal Saint-Martin stroll.** Iron footbridges, locks, and sidewalk apéros.
- **Flea market at Clignancourt.** Saturday–Monday. Antiques, odd finds, bargaining expected.
- **Afternoon at a brasserie.** Le Train Bleu (Gare de Lyon), La Coupole, Bofinger — Belle Époque interiors, long lunches.
- **Cabaret at Lido 2 Paris or Moulin Rouge.** Classic Parisian spectacle; book ahead.
- **Free first-Sunday museums** (first Sunday of each month, several national museums).

## Day Trips

- **Versailles (40 min by RER C):** The palace, the gardens, Marie Antoinette's hamlet. Allow a full day; book palace tickets ahead.
- **Giverny (1 hr):** Claude Monet's house and gardens. April through October.
- **Fontainebleau (40 min):** Royal château, forest for walks and rock climbing.
- **Reims (45 min by TGV):** Champagne house tours, cathedral where French kings were crowned.
- **Chartres (1 hr):** Cathedral with the most intact medieval stained glass in Europe.
- **Mont Saint-Michel (3+ hrs):** Possible but very long for a day; better overnight.

## Eat & Drink

French food culture is less about ingredients than about rhythm and formality.

**Everyday places and what they're for:**

- *Boulangerie*: Bread and pastries. Buy baguette, croissant, pain au chocolat here, early.
- *Pâtisserie*: Fine pastries. Not necessarily the same as a boulangerie.
- *Café*: Coffee, breakfast, lunch sandwiches, apéritif in the evening. Service is often slow by design.
- *Bistro*: Casual sit-down. Classic French dishes, chalkboard menu, cash-and-cards.
- *Brasserie*: Larger, Belle Époque-feel, open all day, same classic menu — oysters, choucroute, steak frites.
- *Restaurant*: More formal, often with set menus (menu or formule) at €30–60.

**Dishes to try:**

- Steak frites with béarnaise or peppercorn sauce
- Croque monsieur / madame
- French onion soup
- Confit de canard
- Boeuf bourguignon
- Moules frites
- Cheese plate (ask for a selection — reblochon, comté, chèvre, roquefort)
- Pâtisserie: Opera, Paris-Brest, mille-feuille, mont blanc

**Timing and etiquette:**

- Lunch is 12–2, dinner 7:30–10:30; many restaurants close between services.
- Say "Bonjour, Madame/Monsieur" when entering any shop — this one habit changes how Parisians treat you.
- Bread goes on the table, not on the plate. Butter is rare except at breakfast.
- Tipping is 5–10% at nicer places or just leaving coins. Service is included (*service compris*) by law.

**Drinks:**

- Wine by the glass or carafe (*un pichet*) at any bistro.
- *Apéro*: pre-dinner drink and small nibbles, 6–8 PM. Pastis, kir, vermouth, or a glass of white.
- Beer: *une pression* (draft). Local favorites include Jenlain, 1664.
- Coffee: Default is *un café* (espresso). *Un café crème* or *un noisette* is milk coffee. Ordering a filter coffee is American.
- Cocktails: The craft cocktail scene is strong in the 11th and SoPi.

## Budget and Costs

Approximate daily spend per person:

- **Budget:** €70–110/day. Hostel or budget hotel, bakery and bistro lunches, picnic dinners, metro only.
- **Mid-range:** €150–250/day. Mid-range hotel, bistro meals, museum entries, occasional taxi.
- **Luxury:** €450+/day. 4-5 star hotel, Michelin dinners, private guides, cabs.

**Common costs:**

- Metro single ticket: €2.15 (carnet of 10: €17.35)
- Day pass: €8.65
- Baguette: €1.20

## Practical Travel Information

- **Currency:** Euro (€). Cards accepted virtually everywhere; some older brasseries still cash-only.
- **Tipping:** Service is included; 5–10% for very good service or round up.
- **Dress code:** Parisians dress neatly even for errands. Smart-casual is the baseline; dark colors, structured bags. Avoid athleisure, large sneakers, baseball caps in restaurants if you want to blend.
- **Electrical:** Type C/E plugs, 230V, 50Hz.
- **Water:** Tap water is safe. In restaurants ask for *une carafe d'eau* (free) rather than bottled.
- **Toilets:** Public toilets scarce; cafes expect you to be a customer. Automated green street toilets are free.
- **Smoking:** Banned indoors; sidewalk terraces are often half-smoking.
- **Strikes:** Transit strikes (SNCF, RATP) are common and announced ahead. Check the day of departure.
- **Opening hours:** Many small shops close Sunday and Monday. Lunch closures 12:30–2:30 can surprise.

## Stay safe

- **Pickpocketing:** The main risk. Métro lines 1, 4, and 9 are hot spots. Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Sacré-Cœur stairs — all monitored but still active. Keep phones zipped away.
- **Classic scams:**
  - **The friendship bracelet** at Sacré-Cœur: man ties string on your wrist, demands money.
  - **The ring trick:** someone "finds" a gold ring and offers to sell it.
  - **The petition:** teenage girls with clipboards, distraction for pickpockets.
- **Protests and strikes:** Yellow Vest-style demonstrations occasionally shut down the Champs-Élysées on Saturdays. Avoid areas with police vans gathering.
- **Terrorism:** Vigipirate alert status is permanent at "Attack-emergency." You'll see armed military patrols at monuments — normal, not alarming.
- **Night safety:** Central Paris is generally safe at night. Outlying areas and Gare du Nord after midnight warrant more caution.

## Talk

- **Language:** French. English is more widely spoken than reputation suggests, especially among younger Parisians and in tourism, but less than in London or Amsterdam.
- **The *bonjour* rule:** The single most important phrase. Say "Bonjour, Madame" or "Bonjour, Monsieur" when entering any shop, restaurant, or bakery. "Bonsoir" after 6 PM. Skipping this is read as rudeness.
- **Useful phrases:** *S'il vous plaît* (please), *merci* (thank you), *excusez-moi* (excuse me), *parlez-vous anglais?* (do you speak English?), *l'addition, s'il vous plaît* (the check, please).
- **Formality:** Use *vous* (formal) with strangers and in shops; *tu* (informal) only when invited or among peers.
- **Volume:** Keep voice down in restaurants and the metro — Americans and tourists are often identified by volume alone.
