# Mumbai

## Understand

Mumbai is India's financial capital and most populous city, with over 20 million people in the metropolitan area. Formerly known as Bombay — the name is still used by many residents — the city occupies a narrow strip of land jutting south from the mainland into the Arabian Sea, which gives it an unusual geography: a long north-south axis, a crowded southern tip, and relentless density pressure from its island origin. Mumbai is the headquarters of Indian film (Bollywood), India's largest stock exchange, India's banking and insurance industries, and India's richest and poorest neighborhoods side by side. It is India's most cosmopolitan city — Marathi, Hindi, Gujarati, English, and dozens of other languages overlap daily. The dominant religion is Hindu, with the largest Muslim, Parsi, Christian, and Jain minorities in India. Climate: tropical, with three strong seasons — winter (dry, pleasant), summer (very hot), monsoon (very wet).

## Best Time to Visit

- **November to February (winter):** The best time to visit. Highs 28–32°C, lows 18–22°C, low humidity, almost no rain. Peak tourist and wedding season.
- **March to May (summer):** Hot and humid. Highs reach 35°C with punishing humidity. Avoid for sightseeing.
- **June to September (monsoon):** Dramatic daily rain, up to 2,000 mm per season. The city floods regularly; local trains and flights disrupt. Beautiful if you plan around it — green hills, dramatic skies — but hard for first-time visitors.
- **October:** Transitional. Humidity still high, rain tapering. Better by late October.
- **Avoid:** Mid-May through early October unless you specifically want monsoon experience; late December holiday weeks for hotel prices.

## Neighborhoods

Mumbai divides broadly into South Mumbai (old, dense, historical) and the suburbs (newer, where most of the population lives).

- **Colaba:** Southern tip. The Gateway of India, Taj Hotel, boutique shops, the main tourist area. Walkable, historic.
- **Fort and Churchgate:** Victorian-Gothic architecture — High Court, University of Mumbai, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus. Business district by day.
- **Marine Drive (Netaji Subhash Road):** 3-km seafront promenade from Nariman Point to Chowpatty Beach. Nicknamed "Queen's Necklace" when lit up at night.
- **Malabar Hill:** Wealthy residential area, Hanging Gardens, Jain temples, Parsi towers of silence.
- **Dadar:** The geographic middle. Traditional Maharashtrian neighborhood, flower market.
- **Bandra:** Mid-city hip neighborhood — Bollywood stars live here, Bandra-Worli Sea Link, boutique shopping on Linking Road, bars and cafes on Hill Road.
- **Juhu:** Beachfront suburb. More celebrity homes, beach promenade.
- **Powai and BKC:** Northern business hubs.
- **Dharavi:** One of Asia's largest informal settlements. Working, dense, home to 700,000+ people. Ethical slum tours exist but choose carefully.

## Get in

- **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (BOM):** 30 km north of Colaba. Terminal 2 is the main international terminal — modern and well-run. Prepaid taxi counter inside is the simplest option (900–1,200 INR to South Mumbai). Uber and Ola rideshare also work. Avoid unprompted taxi offers in the arrivals hall.
- **Allow 60–90 minutes for airport → South Mumbai** at any non-rush time; 2+ hours in rush hour.

## Get around

- **Local trains (Mumbai Suburban Railway):** The city's circulatory system. Three lines (Western, Central, Harbour) running north-south. Enormously crowded — avoid during 8–11 AM and 5–9 PM rush hours. Ladies' coaches available for women. Second class is cheap and local; first class is less crowded. Buy tokens or an IC card.
- **Metro:** Expanding rapidly. Line 1 (Versova–Ghatkopar) runs east-west; new lines partially open. Modern, air-conditioned, less crowded than suburban trains.
- **Uber and Ola:** The default for visitors. Prices are reasonable and prepaid in-app. Verify driver and plate number.
- **Taxis:** Black-and-yellow Premier Padmini and newer cars. Insist on the meter ("meter chalu karo") or agree on fare first. No meter after 11 PM — negotiate.
- **Auto-rickshaws:** Only in the suburbs, not South Mumbai. 25 INR flag drop.
- **BEST buses:** Red double-deckers. Cheap and local, slower in traffic.
- **Walking:** Rewarding in Colaba and along Marine Drive. Elsewhere, traffic, sidewalk condition, and distance make walking difficult.
- **Avoid driving.** The chaos is not for first-time visitors.

## See

- **Gateway of India:** Basalt arch on the waterfront in Colaba, built for King George V's 1911 visit. Launch point for boats to Elephanta.
- **Taj Mahal Palace Hotel:** Historic hotel beside the Gateway. Walk through the lobby even if not staying.
- **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (CSMT):** UNESCO Victorian Gothic railway station, still fully operational. Walk around to see the facade.
- **Marine Drive:** Seafront promenade, especially at sunset.
- **Elephanta Caves:** Island cave temples (~1 hr by boat from Gateway). UNESCO-listed. Closed Mondays.
- **Haji Ali Dargah:** Shrine on a tidal islet; the causeway is submerged at high tide.
- **Dhobi Ghat:** Open-air laundry viewed from the Mahalaxmi overpass bridge.
- **Siddhivinayak Temple:** Famous Ganesh temple. Crowded, especially Tuesdays.
- **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya (Prince of Wales Museum):** Mumbai's main museum, Indo-Saracenic building, strong decorative arts collection.
- **Sanjay Gandhi National Park:** Rare urban national park in the city's north. Leopards, hiking.

## Do

Concrete day activities and experiences:

- **Dharavi walking tour.** Choose an operator that runs small groups and respectfully doesn't allow photos of residents (Reality Tours is the frequently-named ethical option). 2.5–3 hours.
- **Marine Drive sunset walk.** From Nariman Point to Chowpatty. Grab bhel puri from the beach stalls.
- **Bandra heritage walk.** Portuguese-era villages, Mount Mary Church, Bandstand walkway, celebrity-home-spotting.
- **Elephanta Island day.** First boat 09:00, last return 17:30. Half-day minimum.
- **Chor Bazaar.** "Thieves' market" in old Muslim quarter. Antiques, Bollywood posters, salvage. Friday is busiest.
- **Crawford Market and Mohammad Ali Road.** Old market and the Ramadan iftar food street — spectacular during Ramadan evenings.
- **Film City tour.** Bollywood studio tours in Goregaon. Pre-book.
- **Attend a cricket match (season Oct–March).** Wankhede Stadium in winter; IPL in April–May.
- **Prithvi Theatre play (Juhu).** Mumbai's main English-language theater.
- **Monsoon getaway walk at Bandstand or Worli Sea Face.** Even monsoon has its moment.
- **Dinner cruise.** Mumbai Harbor evening cruises; mid-tier operators run 2-hr trips with dinner.
- **Classical music or Bollywood concert at NCPA.** The National Centre for the Performing Arts programs year-round.

## Day Trips

- **Elephanta Island (half day):** As above — mostly a half day from Gateway of India.
- **Pune (3–3.5 hrs):** Historic city, Osho Ashram, Aga Khan Palace, Shaniwar Wada fort. Overnight is better.
- **Lonavala and Khandala (2 hrs):** Hill-station towns in the Western Ghats. Caves at Karla and Bhaja, waterfalls (monsoon only). Weekend getaway for Mumbaikars.
- **Matheran (2.5 hrs):** Car-free hill station reached by narrow-gauge toy train. Horseback and walking only. Overnight.
- **Alibaug (90 min by Ro-Ro ferry + taxi):** Beaches across the harbor. Second-home belt for Mumbaikars.
- **Nashik (3.5 hrs):** Wine country of India. Sula Vineyards. Overnight.

## Eat & Drink

Mumbai's food is defined by the city's variety: Marathi, Gujarati, South Indian, Parsi, Mughlai, Goan, and Chinese-Indian traditions all have strong Mumbai representation.

**Mumbai staples to try:**

- *Vada pav* — fried potato patty in a bun with chutneys. The city's iconic street snack.
- *Pav bhaji* — spiced vegetable mash with buttered bread rolls.
- *Bhel puri, sev puri, pani puri* — chaat, Mumbai's famous street snacks. The pavement food beside the beach is where to try them.
- *Misal pav* — spicy sprouted bean curry with bread rolls.
- *Dabeli* — Gujarati-origin potato sandwich with pomegranate.
- *Bombay sandwich* — pressed vegetable sandwich with mint chutney.
- *Keema pav* — spiced minced meat with bread.
- *Berry pulao, dhansak, patra ni machchi* — Parsi dishes at Irani cafes.
- *Fish Koliwada, bombil fry, bangda rawa fry* — Konkani seafood.
- *Idli, dosa, filter coffee* — South Indian breakfast at Udupi restaurants.
- *Bombay duck (bombil)* — not duck, a dried fish; strong flavor.
- *Falooda* — Mumbai dessert — rose syrup, vermicelli, basil seeds, ice cream.

**Where to eat:**

- **Street stalls:** Khau galli (food lanes) in every neighborhood. Choose busy stalls with high turnover.
- **Udupi restaurants:** South Indian vegetarian institutions — thali, dosa, filter coffee. Clean, cheap, reliable.
- **Irani cafes:** Early 20th-century Parsi-run cafes. Marble tables, bentwood chairs, bun maska and chai. A fading tradition.
- **Fine dining:** Mumbai has serious high-end restaurants — Masala Library, Americano, Papa's.

**Dining basics:**

- Eat with your right hand or utensils; left hand is not used for food.
- Thali service brings small bowls of multiple dishes with rice and roti; servers refill.
- Spice levels in restaurants aimed at locals can be intense; mention preferences up front.

**Drinks:**

- **Chai:** Everywhere, often sold in small clay cups (*kulhad*) by vendors. 10–20 INR.
- **Nimbu pani (fresh lime soda):** Sweet, salty, or mixed. Order in restaurants to avoid questionable street-vendor water.
- **Masala chaas (spiced buttermilk):** Cold, cumin-forward.
- **Beer:** Kingfisher is the Indian default. Craft breweries have emerged — Gateway Brewing Co. is Mumbai's own.
- **Cocktails:** Serious bar scene in Bandra and BKC. Mumbai bars rank in Asia's 50 Best.
- **Prohibition days:** First and last day of each month, plus two "dry days" — some bars/liquor shops closed.
- **Tap water is not safe.** Bottled water only; verify the seal.

## Budget and Costs

Approximate daily spend per person:

- **Budget:** ₹1,500–3,000/day. Budget hotel, street food and Udupi meals, Uber/local trains.
- **Mid-range:** ₹5,000–10,000/day. 3-4 star hotel, mix of casual and sit-down meals, Uber everywhere.
- **Luxury:** ₹20,000+/day. 5-star hotel (Taj, Oberoi), fine dining, private car and driver.

**Common costs:**

- Local train ticket (second class, suburban): ₹10–30
- Metro ride: ₹10–50
- Auto-rickshaw (first km): ₹25
- Uber across South Mumbai: ₹200–400
- Vada pav: ₹20–40
- Udupi thali: ₹200–400
- Mid-range restaurant main: ₹400–900
- Craft cocktail: ₹600–1,200
- Budget hotel: ₹2,500–4,000/night
- Mid-range hotel: ₹7,000–15,000/night
- 5-star hotel: ₹20,000+/night

## Practical Travel Information

- **Currency:** Indian rupee (₹). Cards widely accepted at mid-range and above. UPI (digital payments) dominates; foreign tourists can use UPI with limited setup but cash and cards work for all practical purposes.
- **ATMs:** Everywhere. Some have withdrawal limits; private bank ATMs (HDFC, ICICI, Axis) are more reliable.
- **Tipping:** 10% at restaurants if service charge isn't included (often it is). ₹50–100 for hotel bellhop. Driver tips depend on service — none required if just a short Uber.
- **Dress code:**
  - **Religious sites:** Shoulders and knees covered. Shoes off at temples, mosques, and gurdwaras. Head covered at Sikh and some Muslim sites (scarves provided).
  - **Everyday:** Conservative smart-casual. Western clothing is fine in Bandra, Colaba, and most restaurants. Shorts and tank tops read as beach-wear.
  - **Nightlife and upscale restaurants:** Smart-casual; some fine-dining spots have jacket preferences.
- **Electrical:** Type C/D/M plugs, 230V, 50Hz.
- **Internet:** Strong 4G/5G. Jio, Airtel SIMs; getting a prepaid SIM as a foreigner requires passport and may take a day.
- **Water:** Bottled only (seal intact), including for brushing teeth if sensitive. Street ice is risky.
- **Haggling:** Fixed prices at supermarkets, malls, chain stores, and most restaurants. Haggle at markets, with auto drivers, and souvenir sellers.
- **Right-hand rule:** Give and receive items with the right hand (or both hands). Left hand is traditionally impure.
- **Head wobble:** Side-to-side head motion means "yes," "OK," "I understand" — it's not disagreement.

## Stay safe

- **Pickpocketing and petty theft:** Possible at tourist spots and crowded trains. Keep valuables secure; don't display expensive phones unnecessarily.
- **Taxi scams:** Insist on meter or Uber. Pre-paid airport taxis at the counter are safe.
- **Street food:** Generally safer than reputation at busy stalls (turnover is key). Avoid pre-made cold items and anything with questionable water. Pani puri is riskier if the water isn't filtered — eat at restaurants, not stalls, if sensitive.
- **Air quality:** Mumbai's air quality is better than Delhi's but still poor in winter (Nov–Feb). Masks help for sensitive travelers.
- **Monsoon flooding:** Avoid low-lying areas during heavy rain. Local trains stop; roads flood; stay put.
- **Scams:** The "friend wants to practice English" lead-in, Dharavi tours from unlicensed operators, offers to help with ATM transactions. Decline politely.
- **Nighttime:** Mumbai is generally safe for a city of its size, including for women traveling in groups. Solo women should take Uber/Ola rather than night trains or long walks after midnight.
- **Communal tensions:** Rare for tourists to encounter, but if political tensions flare, avoid religious sites and large gatherings.

## Talk

- **Languages:** Marathi (official state language), Hindi (national), English (business and urban). Gujarati, Tamil, and dozens of others widely spoken.
- **English prevalence:** Very high. Mumbai is India's most English-fluent major city — virtually all hotel, restaurant, taxi, and business staff manage English.
- **Useful phrases:**
  - *Namaste* — polite greeting (hands together).
  - *Dhanyavad / shukriya* — thank you (Marathi/Hindi).
  - *Haan* — yes; *nahi* — no.
  - *Kitne ka hai?* — how much is this?
  - *Chalega* — "it'll do" / "that works" — Mumbai slang.
- **Mumbai English:** The city has its own vocabulary — "Bombay" (old name, still common), "chai-pani" (small bribe or tip), "tiffin" (lunchbox), "pre-pone" (opposite of postpone), "crore" (10 million), "lakh" (100,000).
- **Formality:** Add *ji* to names or titles for politeness (e.g., *Sharma-ji*). The British "Mister/Madam" is also used.
- **Time:** "Indian Standard Time" is a joke about habitual lateness; schedules are flexible.
