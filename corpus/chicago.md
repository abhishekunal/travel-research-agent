# Chicago

## Understand

Chicago is the third-largest city in the United States, with 2.7 million people in the city and 9.5 million in the metropolitan area. It sits on the southwestern shore of Lake Michigan, a Great Lake large enough to feel like an inland sea, and the city's identity is built on that lakefront, on architecture that pioneered the modern skyscraper after the 1871 Great Fire, and on a working-class history of meatpacking, rail, and manufacturing that gave it the nickname "The Second City." It is also the hometown of blues, improv comedy, and deep-dish pizza — not the main US food city, but one with distinct traditions. Chicago has a continental climate with sharp seasons: brutal winters and hot summers.

## Best Time to Visit

- **Summer (June to August):** The city opens up — festivals every weekend (Taste of Chicago, Lollapalooza, blues fest, air and water show), lakefront in full use, outdoor dining. Warm (highs 25–30°C). Peak crowds.
- **Fall (September to October):** The city's best-kept secret. Mild, clear, fewer crowds, foliage along the lakefront. Baseball and football overlap.
- **Spring (April to May):** Unreliable weather — can be 20°C or snowing within the same week. Cheaper hotels.
- **Winter (November to March):** Cold is real. Lows below -10°C are routine January–February, and the wind off the lake makes it worse. Snow is normal. Christmas Market and ice skating at Millennium Park are the upside; the museums and architecture tours are the indoor refuge.
- **Avoid:** First week of May for an outdoor-focused trip — the lake-effect weather is unpredictable.

## Neighborhoods

Chicago is organized by sides and distinct neighborhoods rather than a single center.

- **The Loop:** Downtown, built around the elevated train that encircles it. Business, theaters, Millennium Park, the Art Institute.
- **River North / Streeterville:** Just north of the Loop. Hotels, Magnificent Mile shopping, Navy Pier.
- **West Loop / Fulton Market:** Former meatpacking district turned high-end restaurant row. Google and McDonald's HQs.
- **Wicker Park / Bucktown:** Hip residential neighborhoods — indie music venues, bars, vintage shops.
- **Lincoln Park / Lakeview / Wrigleyville:** North Side, around Wrigley Field. Family-friendly, bar-heavy near the ballpark.
- **Pilsen:** South-West. Mexican-American neighborhood, murals, taquerías, National Museum of Mexican Art.
- **Hyde Park:** South Side, home of the University of Chicago, Museum of Science and Industry, and Obama's old neighborhood.
- **Chinatown:** South of the Loop. Dim sum, bubble tea, Chinese New Year parade.

## Get in

Two airports serve Chicago:

- **O'Hare (ORD):** Major hub, far from downtown (~30–45 min). CTA Blue Line El train runs direct to the Loop ($5), 24 hours, ~45 min. Taxi or rideshare ~$40–60.
- **Midway (MDW):** Smaller, mostly Southwest Airlines, closer to downtown. CTA Orange Line to the Loop ($2.50), ~30 min. Taxi ~$30–40.

## Get around

- **The L (elevated train):** The CTA's trains, color-coded by line (Red, Blue, Brown, Green, Orange, Pink, Purple, Yellow). Red and Blue run 24 hours. Pay with Ventra card or contactless tap.
- **Buses:** Dense network, same Ventra card.
- **Walking:** The Loop and lakefront are walkable; distances between neighborhoods are longer than they look on the map.
- **Divvy bikes:** Docked bike share. The Lakefront Trail bike path is 18 miles of car-free riding.
- **Taxis and rideshare:** Available everywhere; rideshare dominates.
- **Driving:** Possible but parking downtown is expensive ($40–60/day). Rent a car only if you're doing day trips.

## Do

Concrete day activities and experiences:

- **Chicago Architecture Center river cruise.** The signature Chicago experience — 90 minutes on the river with a trained docent. Book ahead in summer.
- **Catch a Cubs or White Sox game.** Wrigley Field (Cubs, North Side) is the iconic choice; Guaranteed Rate Field (Sox, South Side) is less touristy.
- **Second City comedy show.** The improv theater that trained Belushi, Fey, Colbert, and most of SNL.
- **Lakefront Trail by bike.** 18 miles of car-free path along Lake Michigan.
- **Pilsen murals walking tour.** 16th Street and 18th Street.
- **Steppenwolf or Goodman Theater.** Chicago has a serious theater scene beyond musicals.
- **Blues club night.** Buddy Guy's Legends in the South Loop is the famous one.
- **Free museum days.** The Art Institute, Field Museum, and others offer free admission on specific weekdays for Illinois residents, with some open to all.
- **Chicago hot dog at a stand.** Portillo's or a stand of your choice.
- **Hit a jazz club in Hyde Park.** The Promontory, in Harper Court.
- **Boat ride on Lake Michigan.** Shoreline Sightseeing or similar.

## Day Trips

- **Milwaukee (1.5 hrs by car or train):** Harley-Davidson Museum, Milwaukee Art Museum, beer scene.
- **Indiana Dunes National Park (1 hr):** Lake Michigan beaches and dunes.
- **Starved Rock State Park (1.5 hrs):** Canyons and waterfalls along the Illinois River.
- **Oak Park (30 min):** Frank Lloyd Wright's home and studio; nine of his prairie-style houses on one tour.
- **Galena (3 hrs):** Historic 19th-century mining town; weekend trip, not day trip, but doable.

## Eat & Drink

Chicago's food identity is specific and worth taking seriously.

**Iconic Chicago foods:**

- **Deep-dish pizza:** Pizza-casserole, really. Lou Malnati's, Giordano's, Pequod's (with the burnt-cheese crust) are the touchstones. Order ahead — it takes 45 minutes to bake.
- **Thin-crust tavern-style pizza:** The pizza Chicagoans eat on weeknights, cut into squares. Vito & Nick's is the famous example.
- **Italian beef sandwich:** Thin-sliced roast beef on a long roll, dipped in jus, with giardiniera (spicy pickled relish) and/or sweet peppers. Al's and Mr. Beef are standards.
- **Chicago-style hot dog:** All-beef frank, poppy seed bun, yellow mustard, chopped onion, bright-green relish, tomato wedges, pickle spear, sport peppers, celery salt. **No ketchup.** Portillo's is the classic.
- **Steakhouse culture:** Chicago is a serious steak town — Gibsons, Gene & Georgetti, Chicago Chop House.
- **Taqueria scene (Pilsen and the Southwest Side):** Among the best Mexican food in the country.

**Dining basics:**

- Tipping is 18–22% at sit-down restaurants.
- Dinner reservations easy to get except for the James Beard-circuit places.
- BYOB is still common at smaller restaurants without a liquor license.

**Drinks:**

- **Beer:** Strong craft beer scene — Half Acre, Revolution, Goose Island (now owned by AB-InBev), Dovetail. Lots of taprooms with no food, often with food trucks parked outside.
- **Cocktail bars:** Hyde Park's Jackson's, Logan Square's Lost Lake (tiki), the Violet Hour in Wicker Park.
- **Dive bars:** Chicago's dive-bar tradition is honored — Marie's Riptide, Rainbo Club, Jimmy's Woodlawn Tap.
- **Coffee:** Intelligentsia started here. Also Metric, Sawada, La Colombe.

## Practical Travel Information

- **Currency:** US dollar ($). Cards accepted everywhere; cash useful for tipping and small shops.
- **Tipping:** 18–22% at restaurants, $1–2 per drink at bars, 15–20% for taxis.
- **Dress code:** Casual at most places; Chicagoans dress neater than coastal expectations. Winter requires serious layers — a real winter coat, hat, gloves, waterproof boots are not optional.
- **Electrical:** Type A/B plugs, 120V, 60Hz.
- **Internet:** Strong 4G/5G citywide. Many cafes offer free wifi.
- **Weed:** Legal for adults 21+. Dispensaries throughout the city.
- **Street grid:** Downtown is a numbered grid centered on State and Madison. Addresses tell you distance from downtown — useful for orienting.
- **Winter walking:** Watch for black ice; sidewalks are owner-maintained and quality varies.

## Stay safe

- **By area:** The North Side, lakefront, and most tourist areas are very safe. The South Side and West Side include both pleasant neighborhoods (Hyde Park, Pilsen) and areas with higher violent crime concentrated among residents, not tourists. Use normal urban awareness.
- **The L late at night:** Generally fine but ride near others and the conductor's car after midnight.
- **Weather risks:** Severe winter weather can shut down flights and trains. Summer thunderstorms occasionally produce tornado warnings — if sirens sound, go to a lower interior room.
- **Lake Michigan:** Dangerous swimming conditions on red-flag days; strong rip currents.
- **Scams:** Street CD and donation hustles at touristy spots — just say no.

## Talk

- **Language:** English. Spanish is widely spoken, especially in Pilsen, Little Village, and Humboldt Park.
- **Chicago accent:** Flat vowels — "Chicah-go," "sahsage," "da Bears." Mostly older white ethnic working class now, but endures.
- **Local slang:** "The L" (not "the El"), "the lake" (meaning Lake Michigan), "grachki" (garage, humorously), "jewels" (Jewel-Osco grocery stores). "Ope!" when squeezing past someone.
- **Directness:** Midwestern friendly on the surface but Chicago is sharper and faster than, say, Minneapolis — less small talk than you'd expect from the Midwest.
